## CONSOLIDATED OPERATIONAL KNOWLEDGE

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is a YDM certificate the same as copyright registration?**  
A: No. A YDM certificate is a user-declared documentation record. Copyright registration with a government authority (e.g., U.S. Copyright Office, Indonesia's DJKI) provides legally recognized protection. YDM documents evidence; governments register rights.

**Q: Can I use YDM as evidence in court?**  
A: YDM-generated records may have evidentiary value as documentation of a creator's claims. However, their admissibility and weight in legal proceedings depends on jurisdiction, the quality of underlying evidence, and judicial discretion. Always consult legal counsel for use in formal proceedings.

**Q: Does YDM protect me in countries I haven't registered in?**  
A: Copyright protection generally applies automatically in all Berne Convention member countries (179 nations). YDM documentation supports that protection but does not create it. Formal registration in key markets provides stronger legal position.

**Q: What do I do if the other party ignores my YOUR DEAD MEAT Notice?**  
A: Follow the ESCALATION_MATRIX. Options include platform takedown (if applicable), engaging qualified legal counsel, or pursuing formal legal action. Document the non-response as part of your case record.

**Q: Can AI help me write my provenance documents retroactively?**  
A: AI can help format and structure documentation, but it cannot create evidence where none exists. Retroactive documentation based on memory is weaker than contemporaneous records. Document accurately what you have; don't fabricate.

**Q: My client says they own the work because they paid me. Are they right?**  
A: This depends on jurisdiction, the terms of your agreement, and whether the work qualifies as "work made for hire." In many countries, freelancers retain copyright unless there's a written assignment. This is why written contracts are essential. Consult a qualified IP attorney.

**Q: How long should I keep my evidence?**  
A: See DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md. As a general rule: keep creator records and provenance evidence indefinitely for high-value work. Keep case investigation records for the limitation period applicable in your jurisdiction plus a margin.

### Quick Reference — Key IP Concepts

| Term | Plain Language Meaning |
|------|----------------------|
| Copyright | Automatic right to control how your creative work is copied and used |
| Trademark | Brand identifier protection — prevents others using confusingly similar marks |
| Patent | Exclusive right to an invention, requiring formal filing |
| Trade secret | Protection for confidential business information |
| Infringement | Using protected IP without permission |
| License | Permission granted to use IP under specific conditions |
| Assignment | Transfer of IP rights from one party to another |
| Work made for hire | Work owned by employer/commissioner rather than creator |
| Moral rights | Creator's right to attribution and to object to derogatory treatment |
| Fair use | Permitted use of copyrighted material without license (jurisdiction-specific) |
| Derivative work | Work based on a pre-existing protected work |
| Public domain | Works no longer protected by copyright (usually very old works) |
